Purchasing

What format are the patterns in?

Every Stitchfolk pattern is a PDF, formatted for print and screen. Buy it, download it, and it is yours to keep — open it on a phone or tablet at the craft table, or print it and mark it up the way makers have always marked up patterns.

Do you offer refunds on digital patterns?

Because a PDF is delivered the moment checkout completes, a completed digital sale is final once delivery begins — except where the file is inaccessible, defective, duplicated, or materially not what was described. Then we fix it: a fresh link, a corrected file, a replacement, or a refund as appropriate. The full terms are in our digital downloads & refunds policy.

What devices can open the PDF?

Anything with a PDF reader: phones, tablets, e-readers, and computers, on any current browser. Save the file to a device you control so it is there when the yarn comes out.

How do I reach support?

Through the contact page — a real person reads every message and replies within a day or two. For an order problem, include the email you checked out with and the pattern title; that gets you helped fastest.

Files & downloads

How do I download my pattern after purchase?

Checkout runs through Stripe. The moment payment confirms, your delivery email carries a signed download link for your pattern. Nothing ships and nothing waits — most makers are casting on within the hour.

Where do I find my downloads later? (Can I re-download?)

Save the PDF when it arrives; that copy is yours for good. Download links expire after a while for security. If yours has lapsed, write to us with your order email and we will send a fresh one.

Can I share the PDF with a friend?

Please don’t. Each purchase is a personal-use licence: make the project, print the pattern, give away or sell the finished object — but the PDF itself stays with you. If a friend loves it, the kindest route is their own copy; patterns start at $2.99. The terms of sale spell it out.

Skill levels & the catalog

How do I pick my skill level?

Pick the level you are comfortable with right now, not where you would like to be next year: Beginner (first few projects), Easy (the basic stitches feel automatic), Intermediate (cables, colorwork, lace, and shaping), Experienced (any technique in the catalog). Making & materials

What is yarn weight and why does it matter?

Yarn weight is the thickness of the strand — from lace to chunky. A pattern is written for one weight; swap in a very different weight and the finished piece changes size, drape, and how much yarn it drinks. Match the weight on the pattern (or swatch carefully) and the math behaves.

Can I use a crochet hook instead of knitting needles (or vice versa)?

They are different crafts making different fabric — a crochet pattern worked with needles is not a shortcut, it is a different project. Every pattern on the shelf names its craft; start from the crochet, knitting, or needlepoint shelves and you will always be holding the right tool.

What does "gauge" mean on a pattern?

Gauge is how many stitches and rows fit in a measured square — usually 4 inches. It is the difference between a hat that fits and a hat for a giant. A ten-minute swatch before you start tells you whether your tension matches the pattern’s, while there is still time to change needle or hook size.
