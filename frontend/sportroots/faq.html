Youth sports drills — parent questions

The questions parents actually ask — planned plans (paid enrollment opening soon), age bands, devices, and privacy. Browsing drills and requesting a coach booking are free today.

Is SportRoots a good fit for beginners?

Absolutely. Every drill library is organized by skill tier — from first-timers through elite club — so your athlete starts where they are and works up at their own pace. Most of our drills are designed to be done at home with zero equipment.

What ages does it cover?

SportRoots is built for athletes ages 6 to 18. Programs are sorted into the drill library’s four age bands — U6–U8 (ages 6–8), U10–U12 (ages 10–12), U14–U16 (ages 14–16), and U18 (ages 17–18) — so the drills match your athlete’s stage of development rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.

How is this different from YouTube?

YouTube gives you a jumble of unfiltered clips from strangers. SportRoots gives you one structured drill library: 718 drills grouped by sport, age band, and skill level, so you can see what fits your athlete's stage instead of guessing from search results. No ads and no hunting for the right video.

Do we need any equipment?

No equipment required to start. Most drills use a ball and a small patch of yard or living-room space. When a drill does need a goal, cone, or resistance band, we flag it up front so you know what to grab.

How is my athlete’s screen time managed?

Sessions are short — most drills run 10 to 25 minutes — and the work happens with the athlete on their feet, not sitting still. Parents pick the drills, so screen time stays on your terms — nothing here autoplays, and there are no kid accounts or notifications today.

Can multiple kids in one family use it?

Yes. When paid plans open, one plan will cover the whole household, so siblings in different sports and age groups can train on their own profiles without paying double.

What do the four age bands actually cover?

There are four age bands, matching the drill library. U6–U8 (beginner, ages 6–8) is built around fundamentals, coordination, and body awareness — first-touch drills, balance and footwork foundations, and short sessions that match a younger athlete's attention span. U10–U12 (intermediate, ages 10–12) is sport-specific skill building — first passes of stamina, sequencing of more complex skills, and named youth programs that progress week to week. U14–U16 (advanced, ages 14–16) is competitive preparation — higher-intensity conditioning, advanced game-read drills, and pathways into the U18 progressions. U18 (elite-track continuation, ages 17–18) extends the advanced-track work into higher-intensity game-application and advanced conditioning — the same age band the school and showcase team calendars map onto, so an athlete's drill library continues advancing instead of topping out at U14–U16.

What's supported — phones, tablets, and browsers?

SportRoots is a web app, so there is no native download to install. It runs on modern iPhone and iPad (current iOS plus the two prior major versions), modern Android phones and tablets (current OS plus the two prior major versions), and desktop browsers Safari, Chrome, Firefox, and Edge on the same current-plus-two-major-versions basis. Camera, microphone, and location are never needed to browse the library; if a future feature ever asks for one, your browser will ask you first. On an older device or browser, pages may load more slowly and some display details may not work.

Can we upload clips of our athlete?

Not today. SportRoots has no clip upload, no athlete profiles, and no public posting of kids' videos on the site — drills are picked by parents and run at home, at practice, or wherever you train. If uploads are added later, parents will control who can see each clip, and this page will spell out exactly how that works before it launches.
